James D. May Books · Design System
10 · Voice

Voice

One page. Anyone writing copy in this system reads it before they write, not after.

Mechanics

Plain sentences. Subject, verb, object. If a sentence needs a second comma to hold together, it is two sentences.

No em dashes anywhere. A comma, a full stop, or a colon does the work.

No exclamation points. Not in social, not in email subject lines, not in a retailer blurb.

Numerals as numerals. 4,000 years, not four thousand years, outside the novels.

Never these words

grippingunputdownablerelentlesspulse-poundingmust-readheart-stoppingbreaknecktour de force

They are the vocabulary of a book that has nothing specific to say about itself. Say the specific thing instead.

Only if true

bestsellingonly when there is a list and a date to point at
award-winningonly when the award has a name
acclaimedonly with a named outlet quoted underneath

If the claim cannot survive being asked for a source, it does not go on the cover, the site, or an ad.

The two rules that get broken

Don’t

What if everything you thought you knew about the intelligence community was a lie? A pulse-pounding, unputdownable thriller you will not be able to put down.

Do

Miranda lost her badge over a report nobody wanted filed. A man she has never met offers it back, along with a job she is not cleared to understand. He has been doing that job since before the agency existed.

Book copy opens on something that happened. Never on a question. A question asks the reader to do the work of caring before you have given them a reason to. Voice demonstration only, not approved jacket copy.

Don’t

James D. May is a renowned author of gripping espionage thrillers that have captivated readers around the world.

Do

James D. May [SPENT X YEARS DOING Y]. He started writing Oracle Veil after [CONCRETE EVENT]. He lives in [PLACE].

The author is described by what he did. Not by what he is, not by what readers allegedly felt. The brackets are real: nobody involved in this document knows the biography, and inventing one for an author brand is the single worst thing you could do with this page.

Test a sentence

Could it describe a different book? Then it describes nothing. Cut it.

Does it tell the reader how to feel? Cut the instruction and keep the event.

Does it survive being read flat, out loud, by someone who does not care? That is the only test that matters. The tone words are cold and procedural. Copy that needs enthusiasm to work is off brand.

Length by surface

Retailer blurb110 to 150 words. Three paragraphs. Opens on an event.
Site heroOne sentence, 20 words or fewer.
Email subjectSix words. No colon, no question mark.
Social captionTwo lines. It is a caption, not a paragraph.
Author bio, short25 words. Two facts and the series name.

This page governs marketing copy only

The novels have their own style guide and it does not answer to this one. Oracle Veil runs on fragments, hard cuts, and untagged italic thought. None of that belongs in a newsletter and none of these rules belong in a chapter.
