What breaks and what to do

Forced dark modeApple Mail and Outlook iOS invert a light email. This one is already dark, so the risk runs the other way. Declare <meta name="color-scheme" content="dark light"> and supported-color-schemes, then supply the light values above so a forced flip lands somewhere designed.
Gmail AndroidForce-inverts regardless of what you declare. A transparent bone wordmark disappears. Bake the ink plate into the PNG so the mark carries its own background.
Outlook WindowsWord rendering engine. No background images, no flexbox, no CSS grid. Use nested tables with bgcolor attributes, not CSS background shorthand. The square corners are an advantage here: nothing to fake.
The buttonPadded table cell with bgcolor, never a background image and never a styled anchor on its own. 15px vertical padding on 14px text gives 44px of tap. Add VML for Outlook if the cell fill still drops.
Images offThe wordmark PNG needs alt text reading James D. May Books in bone on ink, so the header still reads as a brand when it fails to load.
TextureNone. No grain, no halftone, no distress. Clients rescale background images or render them as an attachment. Flat ink only.

One red per email

The 2px rule under the header and the button fill are both signal, which is two. Keep the rule, since it is the thing that reads at a glance in an inbox preview, and the button earns the second because it is the only action. This is the one place in the system outside a cover where signal appears twice, and it is deliberate. Nothing else in the email gets red.

Gap · no ESP is chosen

These specs assume hand-built HTML. If the newsletter runs on a drag and drop builder, most of the above becomes unenforceable and the practical answer is a locked template built once by someone who knows the tool. Decide the platform before anyone builds this.
