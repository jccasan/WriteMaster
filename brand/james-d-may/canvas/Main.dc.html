What the source of truth is

Every value in this system was taken off the Oracle Veil Book 1 front cover and the approved wordmark. Where the two disagree, the cover wins. Where both are silent, the choice is restraint and the gap is marked rather than filled.

Conflict 1 · the eagle is already on the cover

The anti-reference list bans eagles. The Book 1 cover carries the CIA seal at upper right and that seal has an eagle in it. One of the two has to give. The narrow fix is an exception written for real agency seals on covers only, under 3% of trim area, nowhere else in the system. The other fix is dropping the seal from Darkwell forward.

Separate from the design question: 50 U.S.C. §3613 restricts use of the CIA seal and any colourable imitation of it. Fiction has a real defence and retailers still pull covers over it. Put this in front of a rights lawyer before the Darkwell cover locks. That is a legal call, not a design call, and this document does not make it.

Conflict 2 · signal appears twice on the cover

Section 2 of the brief says signal is never used for more than one element. Book 1 uses it twice: the word VEIL, and the series name inside the top strap. The cover wins. The rule as built: covers carry signal exactly twice, the title word and the series name. Every other surface carries it once.

Correction · the title is not in the middle third

The brief puts the title in the middle third. Measured off Book 1, the title band runs 38% to 73% of trim height, centred at 56%. The cover grid in section 05 is built to the measurement, not to the brief.
