James D. May Books · Design System
05 — Texture and imagery

Surface

Three textures, each with a ceiling. Nothing here is allowed near interface text.

Texture kit

Film grain

Opacity 4% to 10%. Monochrome. Shown here at 9%.
Print, covers, social, UI backgrounds at 3%Never over UI text

Halftone in shadow

Opacity 12% to 22%. Shadow areas only, never a flat field. Shown at 20%.
Print and coversNever in UI at all

Concrete distress

Chips out of the letterform, not a layer over it. Covers and title cards only.
Display type at 48px and aboveNever below 48px, never in UI

Photography and illustration

Do

Desaturated to near monochrome, with one warm accent doing all the colour work. Long lens, compressed depth. The figure is turned three quarters away and the face is a profile at most. Light comes from one source and the rest of the frame gives it up.

Don’t

Four hues competing, none of them subordinate. Wide lens, everything in focus. The subject faces camera and makes eye contact, which turns a thriller into a portrait. Even lighting flattens the frame and there is nothing left for the red to interrupt.

Email gets nothing. Clients strip background images, rescale them, or render them as an attachment icon. A flat ink field is the only thing that survives Outlook.

Gap · there is no photo library

The two panels above are direction diagrams drawn in CSS. They are not reference photography and nobody should shoot from them. The Book 1 cover is currently the only real image in this brand, and one image is not a direction. Before Darkwell art starts, put twenty frames on a board and cut it to six. Otherwise the second cover gets designed off a memory of the first one.

Gap · the distress is approximated

The concrete texture on the Book 1 title was made from a source file nobody here has. What is specified above was read off a compressed image. Get the original texture asset or the layered cover file from whoever built it, or Darkwell will not match Book 1 and the shelf will show it.

Opacity is a ceiling, not a suggestion

Every range here has a top. Grain at 18% stops being grain and starts being noise, and it is the first thing that makes a cover look self published.
