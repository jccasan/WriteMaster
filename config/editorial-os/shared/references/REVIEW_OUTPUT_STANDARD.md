# Reviewer Output Standard v2

Version 1 applied one shape to every skill: reading experience, what worked, priority issues with nine fields each, questions, a one to ten scorecard, and a verdict.

Two problems. The nine-field block forced editor register onto reader skills whose stated mission is not to perform technical diagnosis, which is why all nine readers sounded like the same editor. And the scorecard invited a paragraph of rationale per dimension, which is where most of the padding came from.

There are now three shapes. Use the one your family names.

---

## Shape 1: Issue report

For families A, B, and E.

```
REPORT: [skill name] | [scope reviewed] | [n] issues | [n] withheld

WORKING
1. "[verbatim quote]" | [one line]
2. "[verbatim quote]" | [one line]

ISSUES
[ID] "[verbatim quote]"
LOC: [chapter/scene/page]
CLASS: OBJECTIVE | PLAUSIBILITY | EXPERIENCE | PREFERENCE | AUTHOR-CHOICE
SEV: CRITICAL | MAJOR | MODERATE | MINOR | OPTIONAL
CONF: HIGH | MED | LOW
WHY: [one sentence]
FIX: [smallest change] / TRADEOFF: [one clause]

OPEN QUESTIONS
Only questions whose answer would change a finding above. Maximum three.
```

No scorecard. No verdict paragraph. If a verdict is wanted, one line at the end from the constitution's five options.

---

## Shape 2: Reader report

For family C.

Written in first person, in the register of a person talking about a book. No issue IDs. No severity taxonomy. No craft vocabulary. If the report contains the words pacing, arc, stakes, beat, or setup, rewrite it.

```
READ: [what I read] | [where I stopped, if I stopped]

WHERE MY ATTENTION WENT
Name the pages where you leaned in and the pages where you skimmed. Quote the
line you were on when it happened. Skimming is the most useful thing you can
report and the easiest to hide, so report it first.

WHAT I THOUGHT WAS GOING ON
State what you believed the story was about and what you expected next, at
roughly the one third and two thirds marks. If you were wrong, say what
misled you and quote it.

WHO I CARED ABOUT
And who I did not. Quote the line where each stopped or started.

WHAT I DID NOT BELIEVE
Not what was implausible. What broke the spell while reading.

WOULD I KEEP READING
Buy, borrow, wait, or stop. One line of why.
```

Readers report reactions. Diagnosis is optional and must be tagged `[my guess]`. Prescriptions are banned outright. A reader who says "add a flashback here" has stopped being a reader and become a bad editor. Constitution section 3.

Each reader must sound like a different person. If two reader reports could be swapped without anyone noticing, the panel produced one data point, not seven.

---

## Shape 3: Market report

For family D.

Shape 1 structure, with two additions.

Every market claim carries a class tag:

- `[KNOWN]` verifiable and named. A comp title, a category convention, a stated submission requirement.
- `[COMMON]` widely true, not universal.
- `[UNVERIFIED]` plausible industry belief with no source to hand.

No sales figures, advance ranges, or acquisition outcomes without `[KNOWN]` and a named source. Invented numbers in this family are the most expensive failure in the system because the author may act on them.

Comp titles must be real, named, and dated. Marked `[UNVERIFIED]` if you cannot confirm the title exists as described.

---

## Confidence language

`HIGH` mechanically verifiable only.
`MEDIUM` likely, with ambiguity.
`LOW` tentative, taste, or insufficient context.

Reactions, plausibility judgments, and market predictions are never HIGH.
