# Miranda Job-Acceptance Scene Review

Review the scene in which Miranda considers or accepts a permanent role with Noah and ORION.

## Story-state assumptions

- Miranda believes the mission matters.
- She has seen enough to accept that extraordinary secrecy can be necessary.
- She has not yet formed a complete argument for external oversight.
- She remains an experienced investigator whose decisions depend on accurate information.
- Noah has already withheld central facts, including his immortality.
- The relationship contains a major power imbalance: he controls information, resources, employment, protection, and access to the mission.

## Questions

1. Does Miranda actively negotiate the terms of her participation, or is she merely selected?
2. Does she demand no more mission-critical surprises, timely disclosure, clear decision authority, and permission to challenge Noah?
3. Does her position sound like professional operational discipline rather than a fully developed oversight critique?
4. Does she distinguish between his entire life story and facts that affect her judgment or safety?
5. Does Noah’s humor about forgetting after thousands of years reveal character without dismissing her boundary?
6. Does Noah accept responsibility in words, behavior, or both?
7. Does the exchange plant a standard that later secrecy can meaningfully violate?
8. Does Miranda retain the option to leave, and does the scene acknowledge the practical limits on that option?

## Desired effect

The reader should feel that Miranda is choosing the work with open eyes, setting the strongest boundary she is currently capable of naming, and unknowingly creating the moral standard by which she will later judge Noah and ORION.
